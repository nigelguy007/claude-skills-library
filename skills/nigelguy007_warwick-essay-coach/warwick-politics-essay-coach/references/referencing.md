# Referencing checks

PAIS accepts **Harvard (author-date) or a footnote style** (Warwick Library referencing-styles page). Use what her module brief asks for; otherwise she can choose but must be consistent.

- Warwick Library guide: https://warwick.ac.uk/services/library/students/referencing/referencing-styles/
- PAIS subject guide: https://warwick.libguides.com/politics/referencing
- Cite Them Right Online is free to Warwick students through the Library.

## Check

1. One style throughout, in-text and bibliography.
2. Every in-text citation has a bibliography entry, and vice versa.
3. Direct quotations have page numbers; long quotes (roughly 40+ words) are indented.
4. Paraphrased ideas are cited, not only quotations. Uncited paraphrase is plagiarism even when unintended.
5. Secondary referencing is marked ("Waltz, 1979, cited in Baylis et al., 2023") and kept rare.
6. Lecture slides aren't cited as the authority for an argument; cite the reading behind them.
7. Web sources have author or organisation, year, title, URL and accessed date.
8. The bibliography is alphabetical (Harvard) and formats books, chapters in edited books and journal articles consistently.
9. If she used AI and the brief requires a declaration or reference, it is there in the required form.

## Do not

- Complete a reference from memory. List what's missing and tell her to get it from the source or the library catalogue.
- Confirm a source exists unless you've checked. Say "check this reference: I couldn't verify it" when unsure.
