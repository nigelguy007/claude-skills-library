# Marking rubric

## The Warwick 20-point scale

Essays at Warwick are marked on the 20-point scale (UG modules from autumn 2023). Only these marks are used:

| Class | Marks | University descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| First | 100, 94, 88, 82, 78, 74 | 74–88: very high quality work showing excellent knowledge and understanding, analysis, organisation, accuracy, relevance, presentation and skills; may extend existing debates. 94–100: exceptional or original. |
| 2.1 | 68, 65, 62 | High quality work demonstrating good knowledge and understanding, analysis, organisation, accuracy, relevance, presentation and skills. |
| 2.2 | 58, 55, 52 | Competent work: reasonable knowledge and understanding, some analysis, organisation, accuracy, relevance, presentation and skills. |
| Third | 48, 45, 42 | Limited quality, some relevant knowledge and understanding. |
| Fail | 38, 32, 25, 12, 0 | Below the standard for the stage, with significant omissions and errors. |

Source: https://warwick.ac.uk/services/aro/dar/quality/categories/examinations/marking/ug/

PAIS publishes more detailed criteria in its UG handbook (https://warwick.ac.uk/fac/soc/pais/currentstudents/undergraduate/handbook/marking-criteria/, Warwick login required). If she shares that page, use its wording over the working criteria below.

## Working criteria for a Year 1 politics essay

Score each criterion with a band, then give an overall indicative band. The overall band is a judgement, not an average: relevance and argument count most.

| Criterion | First (74+) | 2.1 (62–68) | 2.2 (52–58) | Third (42–48) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Relevance** | Answers the exact question throughout; interrogates its terms and assumptions | Answers the question; occasional drift | Answers a broader or easier version | Largely off the question |
| **Argument / thesis** | Clear thesis sustained throughout; conclusion follows from it | Clear thesis, mostly sustained | Vague, or appears only at the end | No discernible argument |
| **Structure** | Every paragraph advances the argument; clear signposting | Logical, minor repetition | Follows the readings or chronology rather than the argument | Disorganised |
| **Theory and concepts** | Concepts defined and contested; theories compared critically and applied precisely | Concepts used correctly; theory applied | Concepts loose; theory named, not applied | Misunderstandings |
| **Evidence** | Well chosen, accurate, analysed; comparative where useful | Relevant and accurate | Some relevant, mostly descriptive | Thin or inaccurate |
| **Critical engagement** | Weighs competing scholars; meets the strongest counter-argument; reaches a judgement | Presents the debate and takes a side | Summarises readings one after another | Uncritical |
| **Sources** | Wide scholarly range beyond the core text | Good range from the reading list | Narrow; leans on textbook or web | Very few or non-academic |
| **Referencing** | Consistent and complete | Minor slips | Inconsistent | Missing or wrong |
| **Writing** | Precise, fluent, academic register | Clear, few errors | Errors sometimes obscure meaning | Frequent errors |

## Common Year 1 problems that cap the mark

- Describing what scholars say instead of evaluating it ("Waltz argues... Keohane argues...").
- "This essay will discuss..." introductions that never state the answer.
- Theories used as labels ("from a realist perspective") with no assumptions or mechanism.
- Long historical narrative with no link to the question.
- A conclusion that brings in new material or just repeats the introduction.
- Sweeping claims ("all states", "always") without evidence or qualification.
- Over or under length. Check the module's word-count rules; don't guess the penalty.

## Indicative mark rules

- Always say "indicative": the tutor marks, not you.
- Give the band and the nearest point on the scale, e.g. "2.1, around 65".
- Name the two changes most likely to move it up a band.
- Never inflate to encourage. If it's a 2.2, say so and why.
