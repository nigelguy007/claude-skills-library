# Intake and data request

Ask for all of this in **one message**. Accept partial answers. Whatever is missing becomes `UNKNOWN — needed: …` in the memo and a line in Appendix B (data request). Never fill a gap with an estimate presented as fact.

## A. Business basics (required to start)
- What the business sells, to whom, and how it is found (top 3 acquisition channels).
- Revenue model: commission, fee, retainer, subscription or product margin.
- Team size, and who decides.
- The owner's current hypothesis or proposed direction, in their own words.
- What would make this exercise a success for them (a decision, a brief for a designer, a pitch, a plan)?

## B. Baseline numbers (each one strengthens a section; none are required to start)
| Metric | Used in | If missing |
|---|---|---|
| Annual revenue, split by line | option sizing, threat exposure | exposure shown as a % share only, if stated |
| Gross margin or average commission rate | option sizing | UNKNOWN |
| Transactions per year and average deal value | driver formulas | UNKNOWN |
| Enquiry → proposal → win conversion | threat model (is the funnel being disintermediated?) | UNKNOWN |
| Repeat-client share / retention | moat assessment | UNKNOWN |
| Lead sources by share | discoverability risk | UNKNOWN |
| Cost to serve a typical deal (hours) | automation value | UNKNOWN |

## C. Assets and data readiness (needed before any agent design)
- Where does the proprietary knowledge live? (CRM, email, spreadsheets, documents, people's heads.)
- Roughly how many records, and how complete (e.g. "fees recorded for about 40% of bookings")?
- Consent and confidentiality: can this data legally and contractually be used for AI tools? With whom can it be shared?
- Examples of judgement the business is proud of (a past brief or case where it beat the obvious answer). These are the evidence for differentiation claims.

## D. Market context
- 3–5 competitors the owner sees as alternatives, and any they fear.
- The words buyers use when searching or briefing.
- Any sign that clients already use AI tools to shortlist, compare or negotiate.
