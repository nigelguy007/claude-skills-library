# Market and Competitive Intelligence

## When to use

Use this skill once the problem framing stage has produced a clear question, and the
team now needs an outside-in fact base: how big is the addressable market, and who else
is competing for it. It works from whatever fragmented inputs you already have (market
reports, competitor pricing pages, analyst notes, internal win-loss data) rather than
requiring a single clean data source.

## Instructions

1. Ask for or infer the target market definition: geography, customer segment, and
   product category. State the definition explicitly before sizing anything.
2. Build a TAM/SAM/SOM breakdown. State the sizing method for each layer (top-down from
   a market report, bottom-up from unit economics, or a blend) and the source for every
   number used. Never invent a figure without a stated source.
3. Identify the 4 to 8 most relevant competitors from the inputs provided. For each,
   extract positioning, pricing model, target segment, and one or two stated
   differentiators.
4. Organize competitors into a MECE landscape (for example by price tier and target
   segment, or by business model) so the reader sees where whitespace exists.
5. Flag any gap between competitors: an underserved segment, an unaddressed price
   point, or a capability nobody in the set has claimed.
6. Summarize the sizing and the landscape in a single page: total addressable size, the
   serviceable slice, and the two or three most strategically relevant competitive gaps.

## Example prompts

- "Use the market-competitive-intelligence skill to size the market for [product
  category] in [region], using these three market reports: [paste excerpts]."
- "Run this skill on these five competitor pricing pages and win-loss notes to build a
  positioning landscape and flag any whitespace: [paste inputs]."
- "Use market-competitive-intelligence to build a TAM/SAM/SOM view bottom-up from these
  unit economics, since no market report exists for this category: [paste data]."

## Output

A one-page market and competitive brief: the market definition, the TAM/SAM/SOM
breakdown with sizing method and sources stated for each layer, a landscape grid of the
identified competitors grouped by a clear MECE axis, and a short list of the
whitespace gaps the team should weigh when designing strategic options.
